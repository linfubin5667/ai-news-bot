### DeepMind's AlphaProof Code Verifies Signal's Encryption with Three Million Lines of Lean Proofs

### Demystifying the Autoresearch Harness: Context Folding and Skill Deletion Drive Agent Performance

A new arXiv paper systematically isolates how harness design components affect long-horizon, self-evolving autoresearch agents, studying context management and skill management across MLE-Bench and KernelBench with three model families. The key finding: preserving individually accessible records of past attempts (context folding) outperforms rolling summaries by up to 19.2%, while selectively discarding persistently unused lessons beats consolidating or promoting them to standing rules. The resulting system, MLEvolver, achieves a 72.7% medal rate on MLE-Bench Lite and 94% fast₁ on KernelBench Level 3, substantially outperforming AIDE and OpenEvolve under identical inference budgets. This work matters because harness engineering—not just base model quality—is increasingly where agent performance is won, and the paper provides rare controlled evidence for specific design choices. It offers actionable principles for teams building self-improving research agents.

### Language Model Harnesses as Compositional Generalizers

Researchers propose training inherently neurosymbolic LM harnesses—systems like the Recursive Language Model (RLM)—that externalize and manage context programmatically rather than accumulating it in the neural context window. Under RL post-training, they show that training on short tasks generalizes to tasks 2–64× longer, and training in one domain transfers to novel domains sharing latent structure. Using "surrogate harness training" with synthetic fake tool calls, they trained RLM-27B, which improves Qwen3.8-27B by 26.9% on average across hard long-context benchmarks like CTC-Bench and ExpertLongBench while using roughly 2× fewer inference tokens. The result is significant because it suggests a compute-efficient alternative to brute-force long-context training, framing compositional generalization as an architectural property of the harness rather than the base model alone. If it holds up, harness-centric training could reshape how labs allocate post-training compute.

Source: [arXiv NLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28298)

### Grammar-as-a-Draft Accelerates Structured Output Decoding by Up to 2.8x

A new paper introduces Grammar-as-a-Draft (GaaD), which treats the grammar constraint in structured output generation as a second speculative drafter, merging grammar-forced tokens with the drafter's proposal before each verify step. Because speculative drafters are blind to grammars and repeatedly propose invalid keys and delimiters, GaaD exploits information the grammar engine already possesses, raising average acceptance length from 2.06 to 5.43 on MTP/Qwen3.5-0.8B and delivering 2.66–2.81× faster decoding at batch size 1 (1.14–1.70× at batch size 64). Implemented in vllm-torchtpu and an independent Rust runtime, it composes with any drafter without new kernels. Under mixed traffic with 25–50% structured requests, workloads finish 1.03–1.23× sooner while structured requests complete 2.2–3.2× sooner, though plain-text TPOT rises 7–42%. This matters for production serving economics, where JSON-schema and tool-call outputs dominate agent workloads.

Source: [arXiv NLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28349)

### Measuring and Teaching Agents to Act on Delayed Consequences

Researchers introduce SMB Bench, a calibrated 90-day wholesale simulator with 18 causally paired tasks and an 859-run frontier trace dataset, testing whether agents can notice, attribute, and act on the delayed consequences of their decisions in unseen businesses. Across 16 frontier models, only 3 outperform a fixed buying rule, and models frequently record correct diagnoses in their notes without changing purchasing behavior in time—showing that diagnosis, behavior change, and business improvement are separate achievements. Training Qwen3.8-27B using archived business records and frontier quarters as starting states produced more consistent adherence to customer-protection commitments and reduced severe-loss quarters on unseen businesses, though profit benefits remained uncertain. The work is notable for operationalizing "multi-day ongoing operations" as an evaluation domain, an area where single-turn benchmarks offer little signal, and for exposing how carrying commitments through a quarter surfaces real coordination costs. It reframes agent evaluation around consequence attribution rather than task completion.

### Taking the PULSE of Enterprise Workflows: Persistent Project-State Understanding

A new benchmark, PULSE, formulates persistent project-state understanding (PPSU) as a distinct enterprise agent challenge, comprising 1,573 questions over 30 enterprise project histories with deterministic, time-indexed project state and documented collaborator views. Questions span open loops, project evolution, and view gaps—testing whether agents understand work distributed across heterogeneous artifacts, evolving over time, and unevenly visible to different collaborators. Across retrieval agents, graph- and memory-augmented retrieval, long-context, and oracle conditions, the authors find substantial headroom; retrieval and extraction account for the largest share of the gap, but supplying explicit project state on top of gold evidence yields further gains, showing PPSU cannot be reduced to retrieval. Notably, questions requiring reconciliation across multiple parts of project history remain difficult even with relevant evidence provided. This work motivates enterprise agents that explicitly maintain and reconcile temporally grounded state across sources, actors, and time—directly relevant to vendors building workplace AI assistants.
