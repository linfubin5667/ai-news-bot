### MGMem Proposes Deterministic, Provenance-Preserving Memory for Long-Horizon Agents

Researchers have published MGMem, an agent-memory framework that eliminates generative LLM indexing calls in favor of a deterministic discriminative-parser pipeline built around an incidence graph of recurring mentions. The design directly targets three weaknesses of LLM-summarized memory: cost (thousands of generative calls per index build), non-determinism (inconsistent answers across runs), and provenance erasure (readers see synthesized text rather than original turns). On English multi-session dialogue QA, MGMem outperforms matched-protocol baselines on LoCoMo without any generative indexing, and remains competitive on LongMemEval-S while leading or tying on single-session types in local retrieval comparisons. Determinism and provenance are precisely the properties enterprises need for auditable agent memory, making this a notable architectural counterpoint to the prevailing "summarize everything with an LLM" approach.

Source: [arXiv NLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33174)

## Research & Academic Breakthroughs

### MindGuard2 Advances Mental Health Safety Guardrails with Reasoning-Based, Low-Latency Design

A new arXiv paper introduces a clinically grounded harm taxonomy characterizing risk along three dimensions—what the harm is, who is involved, and when it occurs—while separating harm characterization from intervention decisions. The authors relabeled public benchmarks and found they rarely contain harm directed at the user, which accounts for nearly half of harm in real conversations—a striking benchmark-validity finding. They then trained MindGuard2, a 35B-A3B reasoning-based guard model, using supervised fine-tuning on short reasoning traces plus reinforcement learning with a reasoning-length penalty. MindGuard2 achieves balanced accuracy comparable to the substantially larger GLM-5.3 at roughly 10x lower median latency, with low false-positive rates at high recall. The work advances the accuracy-latency Pareto frontier for safety classifiers and releases the model, synthetic data, and annotations—an unusually complete safety artifact.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33263)

### "Context Scaling" Proposed as a Distinct Third Scaling Frontier

An arXiv paper argues that many deployed AI failures stem not from weak inference but from a mismatch between the state a task depends on and the context available when the system acts. The authors formalize a model as P_θ(d|c) governed by three budgets—training (B_θ), decision-time (B_d), and context-infrastructure (B_c)—and define "context scaling" as investment in the system's capacity to acquire, represent, deliver, evaluate, and persist deployment state. They define effective-context value as a task- and intervention-dependent attribution, separate deployment state from model-visible context, and taxonomize failures into availability, fidelity, selection, and utilization categories, organizing mechanisms into a five-operation context lifecycle. The framing is analytically useful because it makes the model-infrastructure pair—not the model alone—the unit of analysis, and it proposes controlled budget interventions and utility-cost curves for evaluation. Expect "context scaling" to gain traction as a budget line and a research agenda.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33160)
