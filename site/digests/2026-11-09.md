### Forge Decision Context Enables Persistent Behavioral Learning for LLM Agents

Researchers have introduced Forge Decision Context (forge_dc), a system that captures behavioral signals from human corrections and tool-call failures, compiles them into imperative RULE directives, and injects them at the decision points where they apply. The system addresses a fundamental limitation of current LLM agents: they do not learn from corrections across sessions, forcing users to fix the same mistakes repeatedly. On DC-Bench—a new benchmark of 71 scenarios across nine behavioral dimensions built from real agent traces—forge_dc achieves 95% compliance, up from 55% with naive retrieval, while injecting approximately 10x fewer tokens per query. The research identifies representation, activation, and placement as the three central design choices for persistent behavioral learning. This work matters because procedural memory—learning *how* to act—has lagged far behind factual memory in agent systems, and closing that gap is essential for production-grade autonomous agents.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08644)

### Learning Precise Subagent Authorization from Agent Skills

A new arXiv paper proposes deriving subagent authorization policies from Agent Skills, which encode procedural knowledge about the operations, tools, and files required at each step of a task. The core insight is that task outcomes and execution trajectories alone provide weak supervision for determining whether a subagent received appropriate permissions, whereas Skills offer a natural supervision signal. The method synthesizes tasks and step-by-step procedures from Skills, executes them through subagent delegation in interactive environments, and uses verified trajectories to build training data. On ClawArena-Team, the approach improves tool permission precision by 13.4 percentage points and workspace permission precision by 24.1 percentage points, while boosting task success by 11.6 percentage points for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. As multi-agent systems proliferate, precise least-privilege authorization becomes a security prerequisite, making this line of research directly relevant to enterprise agent deployment.

Source: [arXiv NLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08905)

## Research & Academic Breakthroughs

### MCQ Format Mechanistically Suppresses LLM Refusal, Study Finds

A new study provides the first mechanistic explanation for why multiple-choice question formats reduce refusal rates in large language models, examining Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B on the Weapons of Mass Destruction Proxy (WMDP) dataset. Using tuned lens techniques, researchers show that models commit to an answer-letter token very quickly in MCQ format, in contrast to the gradual refusal build-up seen in open-ended formats. Activations on MCQ format lie roughly 3x further from the refusal direction, and ablating the attention heads that carry MCQ answer selection increases refusal—direct evidence that answer-selection competes with refusal. The findings indicate that MCQ safety scores primarily measure knowledge capabilities rather than safety mechanisms, a significant methodological concern given how widely MCQ benchmarks are used. This has immediate implications for AI safety evaluation: labs and regulators relying on MCQ-based safety benchmarks may be systematically overestimating model safety.

Source: [arXiv NLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08639)

### Biomedical Fine-Tuning Erodes Knowledge-Centric Abstention, Audit Finds

A large-scale audit of 25 biomedical domain fine-tuned LLMs against 11 paired base checkpoints reveals that knowledge-centric abstention—the model's willingness to say "I don't know"—degrades after fine-tuning. Defined appropriate abstention, already low in base models (median 8.3% on MedAbstain), falls to a median of 4.2% after fine-tuning, decreasing in 21 of 25 pairs. The magnitude and direction of change are heterogeneous across model pairs, suggesting the effect depends on architecture, data mixture, and fine-tuning methodology. This extends prior work on value-centric refusal to the knowledge-centric dimension, which is arguably more consequential in clinical settings where confident fabrication carries direct patient risk. The findings motivate fine-tuning methods that preserve appropriate caution while improving domain capability—a tension that grows more urgent as biomedical fine-tunes proliferate across healthcare enterprises.

Source: [arXiv NLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08780)
