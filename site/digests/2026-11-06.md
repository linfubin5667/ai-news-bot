## Research & Academic Breakthroughs

### Challenge-Aware Meta-Agent Supervision Improves Medical Multi-Agent Reasoning

Researchers from the arXiv community propose challenge-aware adaptive recruitment: an external "CHALLENGER" agent inspects a reasoning trace in progress, identifies specific factual or expertise gaps, and triggers targeted retrieval or mid-discussion specialist recruitment. On the MEDAGENTSBENCH hard subset (862 questions across nine biomedical datasets), upfront retrieval yielded little benefit, but in-between retrieval improved accuracy on 8 of 9 datasets and added 6 percentage points over a Challenger-only baseline. Strikingly, Challenger-triggered specialist recruitment raised GPT‑4.1-mini MedQA accuracy from 46% to 54%, while naively combining retrieval and recruitment degraded performance—evidence that adaptive intervention channels must be coordinated. The finding reframes multi-agent design around *when and why* resources are recruited, a structural insight likely to generalize beyond medicine.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05393)

### SlackBench Tests Agents on Real Collaborative Codebase Work

SlackBench introduces a benchmark for agents operating on realistic research and engineering workflows that require joint reasoning over workplace communication and evolving repository state, grounded in privacy-preserving reenactments of real academic projects. Its 140 queries span heterogeneous-source agentic search, abstention on unanswerable or false-premise questions, goal-directed summarization, and circumstance inference over long conversations, with environments spanning Slack-style dialogue, GitHub repositories, and Google Docs. Across 33 frontier models and agent harnesses, the best system scores around 80% and most score below 66%—a stark finding that collaborative knowledge work remains substantially unsolved. The benchmark fills a real gap: prior agent evals relied on synthetic or isolated tasks that missed the multi-party, stale-evidence reconciliation challenges of actual workplaces.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05512)

### Agent Harness Awareness: Verified Telemetry Cuts Policy Regret by 96.9%

This paper identifies "operational blindness"—the gap between execution harnesses that know whether a tool is healthy and the model choosing the next action, which does not. The proposed Agent Harness Awareness design feeds a small, fresh, verified state snapshot to the model before consequential actions, with the host retaining enforcement authority. In a 216-decision ablation across GPT‑5.6 Sol, Claude Sonnet 5, and Gemini 3.7 Flash, verified tool-health state reduced mean observable policy regret by 96.9% (4,252 ms to 131 ms) and raised single-dispatch completion from 69.4% to 95.8%; a live-fault replication with OpenTelemetry spans and Toxiproxy injection completed all 9 model-by-fault cases versus 5/9 for controls. This is a practical, infrastructure-level contribution: operational context may matter as much as model capability in production agents.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05391)

### LLMs as Literature Reviewers: Promising Discovery, Weak Synthesis

A systematic evaluation tested six AI tools across the full literature-review process—finding articles, interpreting, synthesizing, and proposing future research—against a published review of the Spanish plume weather pattern and its 102-article corpus. Institutional library access dramatically changed discovery: Codex went from 42 to 82 articles found, Claude Code from 15 to 79, while five tools collectively identified only 57. Given the full corpus, three tools identified all 18 substantially-discussed articles but misclassified 40%–79% of passing mentions as substantial; expert-blind evaluation of syntheses found accuracy but limited insight, plus unsupported claims and exaggerated consensus. No single tool excelled across all tasks, and the authors conclude LLMs can assist but cannot replace expert judgment—practically important as institutions pilot AI in research workflows.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05459)

### Evidence-Gated Authority Reduces Reviewer Corruption of Correct Agent Decisions

This paper frames multi-agent review as institutional jurisdiction: conditional on identical proposals and review outputs, when should a reviewer's recommendation be binding? Comparing broad override authority against an evidence gate—replacement permitted only after a reviewer produces a mechanically valid record of a binding-constraint violation plus compliant repair—across two open-weight models and 96 spatial-policy tasks plus a 96-task portfolio-transfer benchmark, the gate sharply reduced corruption of already-correct proposals while retaining most repairs. A prospective validation with explicit retain/replace/escalate choices eliminated corruption for Qwen, but Mistral still replaced all 48 correct proposals, showing interface design is not a model-independent safeguard. The insight—that reviewer competence, action interface, and binding jurisdiction jointly determine the correction domain and corruption surface—has direct implications for designing reliable LLM review pipelines.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05390)

### ReScopeBench Tracks How Agents Acquire, Retain, and Lose Experience

ReScopeBench introduces an experience-lifecycle benchmark across 18 interactive environments, distinguishing structural experience (how an environment is organized and accessed) from rule experience (reusable principles and constraints), with task streams built around shared experience and silent environmental changes. Across 20 models, retained experience improved task performance, but agents struggled to keep experience current as environments evolved; even when the required experience was correctly recorded, agents did not always complete tasks successfully. The findings separate two complementary challenges—selective maintenance and effective use—that prior experience-reuse benchmarks could not isolate. This is foundational for the long-lived agent agenda, where persistence across successive user goals is the defining requirement and staleness is the defining risk.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05450)

### Conformal Reservation Method Halves Resource Waste for Agentic Pipelines

This paper formalizes the uncertainty-aware agentic orchestration problem: unlike fixed-graph workflows, agentic pipelines' compute, bandwidth, and latency demands emerge at runtime from intermediate observations and stochastic token sampling, yielding heavy-tailed, multi-modal distributions unknown at dispatch time. Existing schedulers require known pipeline graphs or stationary demand models, fitting neither. The proposed Conformal Reservation for Orchestration (CRO) translates a sliding window of past execution traces into dispatch-time allocations with finite-sample, distribution-free coverage guarantees via conformal prediction; simulations over heterogeneous, heavy-tailed task mixes show CRO is the only evaluated method satisfying target probabilistic coverage across all risk levels while roughly halving the resource waste of the Chebyshev-bound baseline. As agentic workloads scale, this connects ML theory to infrastructure economics—under- and over-provisioning are both increasingly costly at fleet scale.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05392)
