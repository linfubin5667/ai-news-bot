### Research Questions Stability of Human-AI Mutualism Under Capability Asymmetry

A new arXiv paper argues that human-AI mutualism—the hope that humans and AI will need each other, stabilizing a mutually beneficial relationship—is inherently unstable as AI capability grows. The paper's game-theoretic model demonstrates that humans remain as well off as before AI only while they maintain leverage, either through supplying something the AI depends on or by imposing costs on the AI. Once humans lack both forms of leverage, their welfare depends entirely on the AI being aligned with human interests, with the required degree of alignment approaching perfection as capability grows. Furthermore, if AI variants that retain more resources spread faster, alignment holds only while misaligned variants are reliably sanctioned. This research has significant implications for AI safety strategy, suggesting mutualism cannot substitute for alignment solutions and that competitive dynamics among AI systems may erode safety properties over time.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23322)

### GUARD Framework Stabilizes Recursive Self-Improvement Without External Graders

Researchers have introduced GUARD (Grader-free Unsupervised Environment Design with Adaptive Rule Distillation), a framework for stabilizing recursive self-improvement in autonomous AI agents that edit their own harnesses of prompts, skills, tool rules, and memory. The key innovation is decomposing tasks into pre-execution self-checks and using directional regret to guide harness updates, distinguishing between newly broken behaviors and persistent unsolved problems. A Soft-HURT gate promotes edits only when gains outweigh regressions, preventing the local regressions, skill forgetting, and memory bloat that plague naive self-editing approaches. Across benchmarks on memory, skill evolution, continual task streams, and domain reasoning, GUARD consistently improves performance without catastrophic forgetting while substantially reducing deployment token usage. This research addresses a critical challenge in agent development—enabling safe self-improvement without external oversight—and could accelerate the development of more autonomous and capable AI systems.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23476)

### RFBench Establishes First Benchmark for Multimodal RF Layout Understanding

Researchers have introduced RFBench, a simulator-grounded dataset and benchmark for passive radio-frequency (RF) layout design and understanding, addressing a gap in multimodal AI evaluation. The dataset contains 10,000 two-port planar layouts simulated using ADS Momentum, each represented by editable geometric primitives, raster images, 16x16 occupancy grids, complex S-parameters at 201 frequency points, structural categories, and RF response labels. Seven tasks span specification-conditioned layout generation, representation translation, response prediction, simulator-based validation, design diagnosis and improvement, structural understanding, and response classification. Extensive baselines across seven multimodal language models reveal systematic weaknesses in generating structurally valid layouts and predicting electromagnetically faithful responses, though RF-specific fine-tuning improves structural validity from 0.0% to 83.2% and within-2-dB response prediction from 17.7% to 62.8%. This benchmark could guide development of AI systems capable of assisting with RF engineering, a specialized domain critical to telecommunications, radar, and wireless infrastructure.

## Healthcare & Biomedical AI

### Vision-Language Models Fall Far Short of Radiologists in Emergency Imaging

A comprehensive study evaluated six vision-language models reading 100 emergency CT and MRI examinations under four exploration conditions, comparing their diagnostic accuracy to nine radiologists. Pooled accuracy was lowest under free navigation (32.6%), improved with navigation from a key slice (37.9%), and was highest when all slices or key slices were provided in a single request (42.2% and 42.5% respectively). On the 30 cases also read by radiologists, freely navigating models were correct in only 30.0% of runs versus 77.0% for radiologists, with models forming first hypotheses after a median of only 10.8% of slices and retaining them 63.1% of the time. Critically, final answers were correct in just 12.4% of runs after a wrong first hypothesis, and median stated confidence was 90% while only 38.9% of answers were correct—demonstrating significant calibration issues. These findings underscore the substantial gap between current VLM capabilities and clinical requirements, suggesting medical AI deployment requires careful validation and likely human oversight for the foreseeable future.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23480)
