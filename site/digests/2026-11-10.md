## AI Agents & Autonomous Systems

### Forge Decision Context Enables Persistent Behavioral Learning in LLM Agents
Researchers have published forge_dc, a system addressing a fundamental gap in LLM agents: they do not learn from corrections across sessions, forcing users to fix the same mistakes repeatedly. Unlike existing memory systems that store facts and preferences, forge_dc captures behavioral signals from human corrections and self-assessed tool-call failures, compiles them into imperative RULE directives, and injects them at the relevant decision points. On DC-Bench—a new benchmark of 71 scenarios across nine behavioral dimensions built from real executed agent traces—forge_dc achieves 95% compliance versus 55% for naive retrieval, while injecting roughly 10x fewer tokens per query. The work identifies representation, activation, and placement as the central design choices for persistent behavioral learning, offering a concrete blueprint for the next generation of agent memory architectures.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08644)

## Multimodal AI

### Subliminal Learning Demonstrated in Vision-Language Models
New research shows that subliminal learning—previously observed in LLMs—also occurs in vision-language models ranging from 2B to 7B parameters. Researchers finetuned teacher VLMs to prefer specific animals or facial attributes, then had both biased and control teachers generate numbers for an unrelated geometric-shape task; students trained on the biased teacher's outputs selected the target preference up to ~10 percentage points more often than controls. Transfer strengthened with more training data and extended beyond preferences to explanation style, with biased students echoing the teacher's vocabulary even when selecting identical images. These findings carry significant implications for VLM training pipelines, demonstrating that seemingly innocuous data can propagate hidden behavioral traits and alter how models justify their choices. The results raise pressing questions about data provenance and oversight in multimodal model development.

Source: [arXiv Computer Vision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08538)

### ChainFlow Introduces Flow-Matching Speculative Decoding
Researchers have introduced ChainFlow, the first flow-matching drafter conditioned on a target model's hidden states for speculative decoding. ChainFlow treats drafting as a short transport over hidden states requiring only one or two flow steps, unlike diffusion drafters that need many denoising steps, while a variational autoencoder compresses states so drafting cost barely grows with target size. Lightweight feedback heads restore inter-token dependencies ignored by independent diffusion predictors, and tree drafting raises acceptance rates. On Qwen3.5 models at matched drafter size and training data, ChainFlow outperforms EAGLE-3 and DFlash in vLLM by 39% and 11% respectively, with gains widening as drafters adapt to new languages. The open-sourced code and drafter models make this a practically deployable advance for inference efficiency.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08669)

### Cancellation Alone Insufficient to Recover Overloaded LLM Engines
A rigorous study of retry behavior on vLLM with three open models reveals that propagating cancellation alone does not restore service after transient overload. With retries enabled, only 12–38% of calls arriving after overload were delivered, and scheduler traces show retries typically started prefill less than half a second before timing out. Aborted executions consumed 79–85% of computed prompt tokens, while vLLM's prompt-token counter missed a third to half of all computed tokens. A retry budget restored service by shedding retries but left many calls undelivered, whereas request coalescing delivered every call unless cancellation had already aborted the running execution. The findings argue that overload controls should be judged by the executions retries add and the calls clients actually receive—practical guidance for anyone operating LLM serving infrastructure at scale.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08684)
