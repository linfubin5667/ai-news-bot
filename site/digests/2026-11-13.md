### Research Finds "Cleaning" an Agent's History Can Backfire When the World Still Holds the Error

A new arXiv paper investigates how compacting, repairing, or handing off a tool-using agent's history affects downstream task performance when the real-world effects of past mistakes persist. On AgentCE-Bench, adding a truthful corrective note beside a preserved error raised recovery from 51% to 66%, while presenting a completed corrective action lowered it below the unedited baseline, from 50% to 44%. On AppWorld, a success receipt for a database write lowered whole-task success from 45% to 28% when the write's effect was absent, but raised it from 38% to 49% once the effect was present—a 27-point swing across two receivers. The findings show that the value of history edits depends critically on whether the external world reflects the edited narrative, with "clean-looking" histories diverging from world state leaving agents worse off than no edit at all. This has direct implications for agent memory management, handoff protocols, and reliability engineering in deployed agent systems.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14654)

## Research & Academic Breakthroughs

### Study Exposes Leaderboard Hacking Vulnerabilities in Preference-Based Model Evaluations

An arXiv paper demonstrates that arena-style leaderboards using the Bradley-Terry model are highly vulnerable to manipulation through strategic voting, strategic prompting, and strategic nomination. Each attack can boost a model by up to 15–20 places in rankings, and combined attacks can elevate a model by as much as 45 places. The vulnerabilities apply to any preference-based evaluation permitting open prompting, voting, or model submission—precisely the design of the most prominent public leaderboards. The findings threaten the credibility of the arena rankings that industry, media, and procurement teams increasingly cite as neutral benchmarks. The authors propose safeguards for each attack vector, and the work is likely to accelerate the shift toward audited, closed-loop evaluation methodologies in the model-assessment ecosystem.

Source: [arXiv AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14769)
